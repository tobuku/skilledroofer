How Much Does a Roof Replacement Cost in 2026?

Real pricing data from contractors across the country. What you should expect to pay for a new roof based on material, home size, and where you live.

A full roof replacement in 2026 costs anywhere from $5,000 to $45,000, but most homeowners land between $9,000 and $15,000 for a standard asphalt shingle roof on a typical single-family home. The final number depends on your roof size, the material you choose, your local labor market, and the complexity of the job.

That is a wide range, which is exactly why this guide exists. Below, we break down pricing by material type, walk through every factor that moves the needle on cost, and share practical ways to save money without cutting corners on quality.

Roof Replacement Cost by Material

Material is the single biggest variable in roof replacement cost. Here is what each option costs per square foot installed, including labor, in 2026:

Material Cost per Sq Ft (Installed) Expected Lifespan
Asphalt 3-Tab $3.50 - $5.50 15 - 20 years
Architectural Shingles $4.50 - $7.00 25 - 30 years
Metal Standing Seam $10 - $16 40 - 70 years
Metal Corrugated $4.50 - $8.00 25 - 40 years
Clay Tile $12 - $25 50 - 100 years
Slate $20 - $45 75 - 150 years
TPO Commercial $5.50 - $9.50 20 - 30 years

For a 2,000 sq ft roof, multiply the per-square-foot cost by 2,000 to get a rough estimate. A mid-range architectural shingle roof at $5.50/sq ft comes to about $11,000. A standing seam metal roof at $13/sq ft comes to $26,000. But keep in mind that the cheaper option may cost more over time - a metal roof at $26,000 lasting 50+ years works out to about $520 per year, while a shingle roof at $11,000 lasting 25 years is $440 per year, plus you pay for a second replacement.

Factors That Affect Roof Replacement Cost

Roof Size

The most obvious factor. Roofers measure in "squares" (one square = 100 sq ft). A 2,000 sq ft roof is 20 squares. More squares means more material and more labor hours. The average American home has a roof between 1,500 and 2,500 sq ft.

Pitch and Slope

Steeper roofs cost more. A low-slope roof (4:12 or less) is easy to walk and work on. Once you get above 8:12 pitch, crews need harnesses, roof jacks, and extra safety equipment, which slows production and increases labor cost by 15-25%. A 12:12 pitch (45 degrees) can add 30-50% to labor.

Material Choice

As shown in the table above, material costs range from $3.50/sq ft for basic 3-tab shingles to $45/sq ft for premium slate. The material you choose has the biggest impact on your total bill. See our best roofing materials comparison for help choosing the right one for your home.

Tear-Off vs. Overlay

Removing the existing roof (tear-off) adds $1,000-$3,000 to the project. An overlay - installing new shingles directly over the old ones - saves that cost but is only possible over one existing layer and is not recommended if there is any deck damage. Most professionals recommend a full tear-off for a longer-lasting result.

Deck Repairs

Once the old roof is stripped, damaged plywood or OSB sheathing needs to be replaced before the new roof goes on. Expect to pay $50-$100 per sheet of plywood, plus labor. On older homes, deck repairs can add $500-$2,500 to the total project cost. This is one reason why a full tear-off, while more expensive, is the better long-term choice - it reveals problems that an overlay would hide.

Permits and Inspections

Most municipalities require a building permit for a full roof replacement, typically costing $150-$500. Some areas also require a post-installation inspection. Your contractor should handle the permit process, and the cost is usually included in their bid. If a contractor suggests skipping the permit, that is a red flag.

Geographic Location

Roofing costs vary significantly by region. Labor is more expensive in the Northeast, West Coast, and major metro areas. Material costs fluctuate based on local supply and demand. Coastal and hurricane-prone areas may require higher-rated materials that cost more. A roof that costs $10,000 in rural Texas might cost $16,000 in the New York metro area for the same scope of work.

Time of Year

Peak roofing season (late spring through early fall) comes with higher prices and longer wait times. Scheduling your project in late fall or winter can save 5-15% in many markets, as contractors offer discounts to keep their crews busy during the slow season.

How to Save Money on a Roof Replacement

Get at Least Three Written Estimates

This is the single most effective way to ensure you are paying a fair price. Get three to five detailed written estimates from licensed, insured roofing contractors. Compare scope of work, materials specified, warranty terms, and total price. Do not automatically choose the cheapest bid - an unusually low price often means corners are being cut on materials or labor.

Schedule in the Off-Season

Booking your roof replacement in late fall or winter (outside of severe weather regions) can save 5-15%. Contractors are more willing to negotiate on price when their schedules are lighter.

Choose the Right Material for Your Situation

The most expensive material is not always the best value. If you plan to sell the house within 10 years, premium architectural shingles deliver the best return. If you are staying long-term, a metal roof's higher upfront cost often pays off over its 50+ year lifespan. Match the material to your timeline.

Ask About Manufacturer Rebates and Financing

Major manufacturers like GAF and Owens Corning run seasonal rebate programs through their certified contractor networks. Some contractors also offer 0% financing for 12-18 months. These programs can significantly reduce your out-of-pocket costs without compromising on material quality.

Do Not Skip Underlayment or Flashing

This might sound counterintuitive in a cost-saving section, but cutting corners on underlayment and flashing is the fastest way to create expensive problems later. Proper ice and water shield in valleys and along eaves, quality synthetic underlayment across the deck, and correctly installed step and counter flashing prevent the leaks that lead to interior damage and premature re-roofing.

Check Your Insurance Policy

If your roof was damaged by a covered event (hail, wind, fallen tree), your homeowners insurance may cover part or all of the replacement cost minus your deductible. Get an independent inspection to document damage before filing a claim, and do not sign anything with a contractor before your claim is processed.

Consider Your Roof as a System

Using a complete roofing system from one manufacturer (shingles, underlayment, starter strips, ridge caps, and flashing) often unlocks better warranty coverage and can reduce overall material costs through system pricing. Our shingles guide covers the top manufacturers and their system warranties.

Frequently Asked Questions About Roof Replacement Cost

How much does it cost to replace a 2,000 sq ft roof?

Replacing a 2,000 sq ft roof costs between $9,000 and $15,000 with architectural asphalt shingles, which is the most common choice. Metal roofing on the same size home runs $18,000-$32,000. 3-tab shingles are the cheapest option at $7,000-$11,000. These prices include materials, labor, tear-off, and disposal. Your actual cost depends on roof pitch, number of layers to remove, and your geographic location. For help choosing the right material, see our best roofing materials comparison.

Is it cheaper to reroof over existing shingles?

Yes, installing new shingles over one existing layer saves $1,000-$3,000 in tear-off and disposal costs. However, most roofing professionals recommend a full tear-off because it allows inspection of the roof deck for rot or damage, provides a flatter surface for the new shingles, and avoids added weight on the structure. Many building codes prohibit more than two layers of roofing. An overlay also shortens the new roof's lifespan by 20-25%. Quality underlayment installed over a clean deck is always the better foundation.

How much does labor cost for roof replacement?

Labor typically accounts for 40-60% of total roof replacement cost, or roughly $1.50-$3.00 per square foot. For an average 2,000 sq ft roof, that is $3,000-$6,000 in labor alone. Labor costs are higher for steep roofs (8:12 pitch or greater), complex roof lines with multiple valleys and dormers, and in high cost-of-living areas. Specialty materials like metal standing seam and slate require skilled installers who charge more per hour.

Does homeowners insurance cover roof replacement?

Homeowners insurance typically covers roof replacement if the damage was caused by a covered peril such as hail, wind, fire, or a fallen tree. It does not cover roofs that have failed due to age, neglect, or normal wear and tear. If your roof is over 20 years old, many insurers will only pay actual cash value (depreciated value) rather than full replacement cost. File a claim promptly after storm damage and get an independent inspection before accepting the insurance adjuster's estimate. Our contractor vetting guide can help you find a qualified inspector.

What time of year is cheapest to replace a roof?

Late fall and winter (November through February) are typically the cheapest times to replace a roof in most of the US. Demand drops during cooler months, and many contractors offer discounts to keep crews working. Spring and summer are peak season with higher prices and longer wait times. However, in northern states with harsh winters, the installation window is limited, so late fall is the sweet spot for savings without weather risk.
